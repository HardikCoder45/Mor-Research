# Deep Note — MobileLLM: Optimizing Sub-billion Parameter Language Models for On-Device Use Cases

**arXiv:** 2402.14905  
**Local PDF:** `../../papers/related/2402.14905.pdf`  
**Topic:** small-model architecture / on-device parameter allocation

## Why it is one of the highest-value papers for this project

MobileLLM directly studies the regime we care about: approximately 125M and 350M decoder-only language models under a fixed storage budget. Instead of assuming architecture differences wash out at fixed parameter count, it trains many controlled variants and shows that *where the parameters go* matters substantially at sub-billion scale.

The four baseline choices that survive its ablation program are:

1. **SwiGLU FFNs**;
2. **deep-and-thin geometry**;
3. **tied input/output embeddings** with saved parameters reallocated to transformer depth;
4. **grouped-query attention (GQA)** with many query heads and fewer KV heads.

It then adds **block-wise layer sharing** as an additional storage-neutral way to increase logical depth, explicitly choosing a sharing schedule that maps well to the small SRAM / larger DRAM hierarchy of mobile devices.

## Training and evaluation context

The exploration stage trains candidate models on about 0.25T tokens. Final reported MobileLLM models are trained for about 1T tokens. Evaluation includes eight zero-shot commonsense tasks plus TriviaQA and RACE.

This is older and much smaller-data training than the strongest 2025-2026 small-model recipes, so its absolute benchmark numbers are not state of the art today. Its **controlled architectural ablations** remain highly relevant because they compare like-for-like models at the target scale.

## SwiGLU is useful even at 125M

Replacing a conventional ReLU FFN with SwiGLU increases the reported 125M zero-shot commonsense average from roughly **42.6 to 43.9**.

**Implication:** do not simplify the FFN to a weak activation merely because the model is small. A gated FFN is still valuable. The correct project question is the optimal `d_ff / d_model` ratio under a fixed parameter budget, not whether to abandon gating.

## Depth vs width: the core result

The authors train 19 comparable-size variants (9 around 125M and 10 around 350M) with different depth/width allocations. Across most commonsense, QA, and reading-comprehension tasks, **deeper and thinner models outperform shallower and wider ones at similar parameter count**.

At roughly 125M, models with about **30 or even 42 layers** can outperform the conventional ~12-layer geometry common in earlier 125M GPT-style models.

### Why this matters mechanically

At fixed block parameter count, widening increases matrix dimensions quadratically, while adding another thinner block increases sequential nonlinear transformations approximately linearly. Small models appear to benefit more from representational depth than from excessively wide hidden states.

For an on-device model, deeper-thin has a latency tradeoff: more sequential kernel launches / dependency depth can increase latency even if total FLOPs are similar. The correct optimization therefore is not “maximize layers,” but **find the deepest geometry that still maps efficiently to the target runtime**, then recover additional logical depth through parameter reuse only when it improves quality per byte and per joule.

## Embedding sharing: a large budget lever at tiny scale

The paper highlights how expensive untied vocabulary projections become in small models. With vocabulary 32k and hidden width 512, each embedding/projection matrix contains about 16M parameters. Two separate matrices therefore consume more than 20% of a 125M model.

In the 30-layer 125M ablation:

| Variant | Params | Avg. zero-shot commonsense |
|---|---:|---:|
| Untied embeddings | 135M | 44.8 |
| Tied input/output embeddings | 119M | 44.6 |
| Tied + saved budget reallocated to +2 layers | 125M | 45.0 |

Tying saves about **16M parameters (~11.8%)** with only ~0.2 points immediate loss; spending part of the saving on more depth yields an overall gain.

### Project implication

For a strict <500M total-parameter model, **tied embeddings should be the default baseline**. Untying requires unusually strong empirical justification.

This also makes tokenizer vocabulary a first-class architecture variable. A giant vocabulary can consume a disproportionate fraction of the model even if the transformer stack is efficient. Compare, for example, a 64k vocabulary at `d=1024` (65.5M tied embedding parameters) with a 256k vocabulary (262.1M): the latter would use more than half of a 500M budget before any transformer blocks.

## GQA: both cache and parameter efficiency

The authors find 16 query heads favorable in their studied configurations. Reducing KV heads from 16 to 4:

- is essentially accuracy-neutral at 125M;
- costs only about 0.2 average points at 350M in one ablation;
- reduces model size by roughly 10% in that setup;
- also reduces decode-time KV-cache footprint and bandwidth.

When the saved parameter budget is reinvested into useful capacity, the 125M model improves further.

### Project implication

GQA is close to a mandatory starting point for an on-device decoder. Our experiments should compare `n_q : n_kv` ratios such as 16:4, 16:2, and possibly MQA 16:1, but should not use full MHA as the default unless a target NPU/kernel makes it unexpectedly favorable.

## Layer sharing and hardware hierarchy

MobileLLM evaluates three sharing schedules:

- immediate block-wise repeat;
- repeat-all-over;
- reverse sharing.

Raw quality is slightly best for repeat-all-over, but **immediate block-wise sharing** is selected for the deployed design because a transformer block can remain in fast SRAM/cache and be executed again immediately rather than being re-fetched from DRAM.

The paper explicitly reasons about a mobile memory hierarchy where on-chip SRAM is much smaller than model storage; moving weights can dominate autoregressive decoding. This makes layer sharing more than a compression trick: **a repeated block can trade additional arithmetic for less weight traffic**, which is favorable when decode is memory-bandwidth bound.

Reported zero-shot commonsense averages:

| Scale | Baseline | Immediate block share | Repeat-all-over |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ~125M | 44.6 | 45.0 | 45.2 |
| ~350M | 49.6 | 50.2 | 50.7 |

The final MobileLLM-LS models also improve over their non-sharing counterparts (about 46.3→47.0 at 125M and 51.3→52.1 at 350M in the paper's table).

### Relationship to MoR

MobileLLM layer sharing and Mixture-of-Recursions point in the same direction but optimize different axes:

- MobileLLM: repeat shared blocks for **all** tokens in a hardware-friendly schedule.
- MoR: repeat shared computation only for **selected hard tokens**, reducing average active compute.

A high-value experiment is therefore to start with an immediate-repeat / Middle-Cycle-friendly physical layout and replace unconditional second passes with an adaptive causal router.

## Strongest transferable conclusions

### Evidence-backed
- Use **SwiGLU** rather than weak ungated FFNs.
- Allocate a sub-500M parameter budget toward **more, thinner layers**, subject to device latency.
- **Tie input/output embeddings** and spend savings on transformer capacity.
- Use **GQA**; KV-head redundancy is wasteful at small scale.
- Treat **layer reuse as a memory-traffic optimization**, not only parameter compression.

### Project hypotheses
- A 32-40 layer `d≈896-1024` model may be a better dense baseline than a 20-24 layer wider model near the same total parameters.
- A 48k-64k vocabulary may give a better quality-per-parameter balance than very large 150k-256k vocabularies unless multilingual/code coverage proves the extra embedding cost worthwhile.
- Immediate recursive reuse of a selected middle sub-stack may map better to mobile caches than recursively traversing the whole model.
- A router should be evaluated against *unconditional* immediate block sharing at the same average logical FLOPs; otherwise adaptive-depth gains can be confounded with simply doing more work.

## Limitations

1. The models were trained on ~1T tokens, below modern heavily overtrained small-model recipes.
2. Benchmark suite is mostly classic commonsense/QA, not modern code/math/long-context/agentic evaluation.
3. Results predate newer quantization and mobile runtimes.
4. The paper does not isolate NPU-specific effects across Apple/Qualcomm/Android hardware.
5. Layer sharing increases logical compute even though storage does not grow.
6. The best sharing schedule for quality differs from the schedule chosen for memory hierarchy, emphasizing that device-aware architecture search is necessary.

## Bottom line

MobileLLM provides unusually direct evidence that **small-model capability is highly sensitive to parameter allocation**. For our <500M target it strongly supports a baseline built from *deep-thin blocks, SwiGLU, tied embeddings, and GQA*, then layering adaptive recursion on top. This is more defensible than beginning with a conventional 24-layer mini-LLaMA and hoping training alone closes the gap.
